# Lou

**Original character by the repository owner.** First mentioned in the Grandma Dot brief,
11 November 2026. **Status: idea.** Everything marked *draft* below is a suggestion for the
owner to change.

## Known so far (from the owner)

- Grandma Dot's **late husband**.
- He'll one day **coach Cribbage**, the way Dot coaches Euchre.
- Dot on Lou: "A hint? Lou never needed a hint. He needed glasses."

## Draft ideas (for the owner to decide)

- **Personality:** the quiet counterweight to Dot. Dry, unhurried and deadpan. He counts
  every hand out loud and never misses a point (he's the reason the Computer "never forgets
  its own points" in muggins).
- **Look:** same art style as Dot (bold black lines, no color). Late 70s, mostly bald with a
  fringe of hair, big squarish glasses he keeps losing (often pushed up on his forehead),
  cardigan vest over a collared shirt, a pencil behind his ear, a well-worn wooden cribbage
  board under his arm.
- **How he appears**, given he's her *late* husband (the owner's call):
  1. As he was: no explanation, he just coaches.
  2. Gently from beyond: a small halo or cloud nobody mentions.
  3. Through Dot: "Lou always said…", with his picture on the board.
- **Possible lines:** "Fifteen two, fifteen four, and the rest won't help you." /
  "Count it again. Slower." / "Dot was right. Don't tell her I said that."

## Art prompt (draft, to use with the Dot sheet attached)

Attach `../grandma-dot/art/grandma-dot-poses-sheet-2026-11-12.webp` so he matches her style.

```text
Using the attached character sheet as the style reference (same hand-inked black line art, same line weight, no color, same proportions and level of detail), create a character sheet for her husband, "Lou."

Lou: late 70s, a kind but deadpan retired machinist who never misses a point at Cribbage. Mostly bald with a fringe of short hair, big squarish glasses (sometimes pushed up on his forehead), a cardigan vest over a collared shirt, a pencil tucked behind one ear. He often holds a well-worn wooden cribbage board with pegs.

Draw him nine times, upper body (waist up), same scale and framing as the reference sheet, plain white background:
1. Neutral, holding the cribbage board, waiting patiently.
2. Leaning in, tapping a card with the pencil.
3. A small approving nod.
4. Looking over his glasses, unimpressed.
5. Pinching the bridge of his nose (a mistake).
6. Counting on his fingers, mouth mid-word ("fifteen two…").
7. A slow, dry thumbs-up.
8. Quietly smug, moving a peg forward on the board.
9. Pushing his glasses up onto his forehead, waiting.

No text, no logos, no borders, no extra characters.
```

If he should appear "from beyond," add: "Give him a small, simple halo drawn in the same line style."
